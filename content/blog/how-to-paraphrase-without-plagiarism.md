Paraphrasing is essential when working with existing content — but doing it incorrectly can lead to plagiarism.

Learning how to **paraphrase without plagiarism** helps you rewrite ideas clearly while keeping your work original.

## What Is Plagiarism?

Plagiarism happens when you copy someone else's work without properly changing or crediting it.

Even small changes — like replacing a few words — are not enough.

To avoid plagiarism, your sentence must be genuinely rewritten.

## What Does Proper Paraphrasing Mean?

Proper paraphrasing means:

- Keeping the original meaning  
- Changing the wording completely  
- Adjusting sentence structure  
- Making the text your own  

It’s not about swapping words — it’s about rewriting the idea.

## Example: Incorrect vs Correct Paraphrasing

**Original:**  
> Technology is rapidly changing the way people communicate.

**Incorrect (too similar):**  
> Technology is quickly changing how people communicate.

**Correct paraphrase:**  
> The way people communicate is evolving rapidly due to technology.

The correct version changes both structure and wording.

## Step-by-Step: How to Paraphrase Properly

### 1. Understand the Original

Make sure you fully understand the sentence before rewriting it.

### 2. Rewrite Without Looking

Try rewriting the idea in your own words without copying the structure.

### 3. Change Sentence Structure

Reorder the sentence or break it into smaller parts if needed.

### 4. Use Different Vocabulary

Replace words with natural alternatives — not forced synonyms.

### 5. Compare with the Original

Check that your version is clearly different but still accurate.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

- Only replacing a few words  
- Keeping the same sentence structure  
- Using synonyms that don’t fit naturally  
- Not fully understanding the original  

These mistakes can still count as plagiarism.

## When Paraphrasing Is Useful

- Academic writing  
- Research papers  
- Blog writing  
- Content creation  
- Learning and summarizing information  

Paraphrasing helps you explain ideas in your own voice.

Paraphrasing without plagiarism requires more than small word changes. It involves fully rewriting the idea while preserving meaning.

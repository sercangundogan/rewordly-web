## Rewordly vs Grammarly: What's the difference?

**The biggest difference is that Rewordly focuses on rewriting selected text directly inside the page you're already using, while Grammarly primarily focuses on detecting writing issues and suggesting corrections as you type.**

Both tools help improve writing quality, but they approach the problem differently:

* **Rewordly** helps you intentionally rewrite, paraphrase, change tone, or fix grammar for selected text.
* **Grammarly** continuously analyzes your writing and surfaces suggestions while you type.

For many writers, these approaches complement different workflows.

## When Rewordly is the better choice

Rewordly is designed for people who actively want to improve specific pieces of text without leaving the page they're working on.

For example, imagine you're writing a LinkedIn post:

**Before**

> I think our team had a pretty good quarter and we learned a lot from customers.

**After (Formal tone)**

> Our team achieved a productive quarter, gaining valuable insights from customer feedback that will guide future improvements.

Or you're replying to an email:

**Before**

> Thanks. I'll check it tomorrow.

**After (Professional)**

> Thank you for your message. I'll review this tomorrow and follow up with an update.

Instead of copying text into a separate website, you simply:

1. Select the text.
2. Click the floating Rewordly button (or use the context menu or keyboard shortcut).
3. Choose a tone or grammar check.
4. Apply the result directly back into the page.

That workflow is especially useful across Gmail, LinkedIn, Reddit, X, Notion, Medium, and many other websites.

## When Grammarly may be the better choice

Grammarly shines if your priority is continuous writing assistance while you're typing.

If you regularly write long documents and want immediate feedback on spelling, punctuation, or grammar mistakes without manually selecting text, Grammarly's always-on suggestions can be valuable.

Many users prefer passive assistance that catches mistakes automatically before they send a message or publish content.
